# Research and product decision

## Competition fit

The [2026 International CodeTheCure Hackathon](https://codethecure-hackathon-26.devpost.com/) has three tracks: Detection & Diagnosis, Patient Experience & Support, and Prevention & Education. This project targets Patient Experience & Support. The preliminary submission asks for an abstract of at most 300 words, working demo or prototype, GitHub source, and a demonstration video no longer than three minutes. Check the official page for current dates and rules. The detailed judging rubric linked from that page was unavailable during research, so no scoring weights are assumed.

## Problem

People with cancer may need to arrange transportation, lodging, and additional funds for treatment. A [systematic review of non-emergency transportation interventions](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35449011/) found an association with fewer missed appointments; this is not evidence of this application's impact. An [NCI guide](https://www.cancer.gov/about-cancer/managing-care/track-care-costs) identifies travel and childcare among costs worth considering.

## Alternative directions assessed

| Idea | Concern | Decision |
|---|---|---|
| Cancer detection from images | Clinical validation, dataset bias and regulatory risk exceed available time | Decline |
| Clinical trial matching | Existing search tools and eligibility depends on detailed medical records and clinician judgment | Decline |
| Symptom triage | High safety stakes and need for clinical oversight | Decline |
| Screening reminders | Screening guidelines and care pathways vary by jurisdiction and population | Decline |
| Practical access planning | Concrete steps for travel, lodging, and costs; referrals can point to existing authoritative services | Select |

## Existing services and scope

Existing offerings include [ACS CARES](https://www.cancer.org/support-programs-and-services/acs-cares.html), [CancerCare Helping Hand](https://www.cancercare.org/financial_assistance), and [Triage Cancer](https://triagecancer.org/cancer-related-financial-toxicity). This project does not claim to be the first resource finder. Its narrower aim is to help someone make a practical plan around a specific appointment, record next steps and costs, and bring them together in one place.

Initial resource coverage is U.S.-focused. [Road To Recovery](https://www.cancer.org/support-programs-and-services/road-to-recovery.html), [Hope Lodge](https://www.cancer.org/support-programs-and-services/patient-lodging/hope-lodge.html), and [CancerCare](https://www.cancercare.org/financial_assistance) are starting points; each provider determines service availability and eligibility. The application does not book rides, verify eligibility, make financial decisions, diagnose, or recommend treatment. User benefit has not been validated with patients or navigators.
